# Evidence Ladder

Use this to weight sources. The exact hierarchy changes by domain, but direct, replicated, well-measured evidence beats indirect or promotional evidence.

## Stronger Evidence

- Systematic reviews and meta-analyses with clear inclusion criteria.
- Replicated randomized controlled trials for intervention claims.
- Well-designed field studies or benchmarks matching the real use case.
- Official standards, specifications, datasheets, and validated measurement methods.
- Textbooks or review articles for settled mechanisms.
- Open datasets or code that can be inspected.

## Medium Evidence

- Single peer-reviewed studies.
- Preprints with sound methods but limited review.
- Observational studies with plausible controls.
- Engineering benchmarks from credible labs or practitioners.
- Case studies with enough implementation detail to evaluate transferability.

## Weak Evidence

- Anecdotes, forum posts, testimonials, and informal reports.
- Company marketing or press releases.
- Blog summaries without primary links.
- Small demos, simulations, toy examples, or cherry-picked benchmarks.
- Expert opinion without evidence or clear mechanism.

## Common Translation Warnings

- Animal or cell studies do not automatically imply human outcomes.
- Simulation results do not automatically survive real-world noise.
- A benchmark does not prove product value unless it matches the workload.
- A plausible mechanism does not prove magnitude or practical effect.
- Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence, but it should lower confidence for strong claims.
- Correlation does not establish causation without design or controls.
